# THE LAST VARIABLE — Differentiation & Inclusion Guide

*Written by a special education paraprofessional. This game is built so struggling students aren't carried — they're required.*

## The core idea: every role matters by design

This isn't a game where the strongest math student wins and everyone else watches. The mechanics make that impossible:

- **Split information.** Generator objectives are built so each Breaker holds one piece of the solution. The student who holds the coordinate or the missing fraction is as load-bearing as anyone else. They *have* to talk to each other.
- **Same power, different tier.** A power fires when its math is solved correctly — but each player draws problems at **their own tier**. A Tier-1 student firing The Medic's revive is just as heroic as a Tier-3 student doing it. Difficulty never gates who gets to be the hero.
- **Different win conditions.** Breakers win by teamwork; the Glitch wins by speed. There's no head-to-head "I'm smarter than you" moment, because the two sides aren't even playing the same game.

## Setting tiers (1, 2, 3)

Each player privately picks a tier at setup. You can set it for them, let them choose, or assign quietly to match IEP goals. **Mixed-tier tables are the point** — students at different levels share one board and pull from different parts of the same deck. No one is visibly "in the easy group."

- **Tier 1 — Building:** single-step problems, scaffolds welcome.
- **Tier 2 — On-Level:** grade-level fluency.
- **Tier 3 — Challenge:** multi-step; this is also what the Glitch always draws.

## Accommodations (keep the math, change the access)
- **Calculators / multiplication charts** for students whose IEP allows them — the goal is reasoning, not recall speed.
- **Read-aloud:** problems can be read to the student; that doesn't change the math.
- **Extra time:** Breaker powers are untimed. Only the Glitch's powers are timed — pair a student who needs untimed work with a Breaker role.
- **Manipulatives:** fraction tiles, base-ten blocks at the table are fair game.

## Modifications (change the demand)
- Reduce the win condition to **2 generators** instead of 3 for a shorter, lower-load game.
- Pre-online one generator to lower the lift.
- Let two students co-pilot one Breaker (think-aloud partner).

## The Decoder is your inclusion lifeline
**The Decoder's *Translate* power** lets a student read a teammate's clue to the table — once per game. Use it on purpose: it's a built-in, in-fiction way to unstick a struggling student **without anyone having to announce they're stuck.** The help is part of the story, not a spotlight on a weakness.

## The Glitch is the challenge seat, not a punishment
The Glitch draws Tier-3, timed problems and plays solo under pressure. For an advanced student who finishes early and disengages, this is the seat that finally challenges them. Rotate it so it's never about being "the smart one" — it's just whose turn it is to be the boss.

## Behavior / regulation notes
- Turn-based and seated — no physical chasing. The "hunted" tension is all on the board.
- Clear roles and a visible turn track help students who need structure and predictability.
- Rotating the Glitch every game keeps social dynamics from hardening around one student.
